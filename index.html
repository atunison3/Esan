SMALL WORDS, BIG HELP

Listen for these.

บ่ baw
“Not” before a verb; a yes/no question at the end.
หลาย laai
“Much” or “very.” Add it to a thank-you or a food compliment.
ข่อย khoy
“I” or “me.” Useful when introducing yourself.

About the language & pronunciation

Isan (also spelled Isaan or Esan) is spoken in northeastern Thailand. These are general, informal Isan phrases for practising with family, not a verified course in one Ubon accent. Local wording, spelling, and tones vary; use Alice’s own speech as your guide.

The Latin sound guides are approximate and intentionally do not mark tones. “aa” and “ii” suggest longer vowels; “kh” is a k with a puff of air. “ng” in nyang is like the end of “sing”; the initial “ny” is a single sound, similar to the ñ in “señor.” Thai-script หยัง is often approximated as yang in Thai voices. Pronouns and polite endings depend on the relationship; these cards use a casual family register.

Common Isan greetings include asking how someone is or whether they have eaten. Standard Thai สวัสดี (sa-wat dii, hello) is also used in everyday life. The lesson starts with an Isan check-in.
